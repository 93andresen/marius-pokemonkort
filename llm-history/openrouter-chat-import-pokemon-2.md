## 3. The winner explained

**Winner: English-language sealed booster boxes bought from US/EU bulk sellers, sold as single units on Finn.no. Largest verified single-unit wedge = Surging Sparks BB (Finn kr 4,200) / Chaos Rising BB (Finn kr 3,500).**

Four mechanisms create the gap — three of them structural and persistent, one cyclical:

1. **Distribution-layer stacking.** `[V]`-consistent with everything I found: Norwegian sealed supply arrives through 2–3 intermediaries (international bulk buyer → EU/US reseller → Norwegian shop/Finn seller). Each layer adds margin, so the *Norwegian asking price for one box* sits at 4–5× the *verified international price of a comparable box* (kr 3,500–4,200 vs kr 755–850).
2. **25 % MVA is a price floor for the retail incumbent, not for you.** `[V]` toll.no: VAT applies to *all* goods including freight, and non-VOEC clearance happens in TVINN. Norwegian shops must charge MVA-inclusive prices *and* carry rent/staff cost. A private flipper selling kr 4,200 with kr 2,075 landed cost avoids the shop's cost base entirely.
3. **Language premium, not product premium.** This is the finding that reverses the naive thesis. Norwegian sell-side for Japanese boxes is kr 695–992 `[V]` — i.e. Norwegian players discount foreign-language product below international wholesale. Same game, same pack count, opposite outcome. **You are paid to take English-language risk, not Japanese arbitrage.**
4. **Unit-thickness / display inversion.** `[V]` Destined Rivals **display** asks kr 9,000 = kr 1,500/box while single-box asks are kr 3,500–4,200 — the Norwegian market is so thin that *breaking displays into singles* is itself the highest-value action, which is only available to someone importing at display prices.

**Is it sustainable?** Partly. The *structural* part (distribution stacking, MVA floor, thin supply) persists — this gap existed for the entire Scarlet & Violet era. The *cyclical* part is the current sealed-boom sentiment (`[V]` evidence: repeated eBay "Pokémon 30th Anniversary / Celebration" live auctions, 9,000–16,300 kr displays listed as normal stock on Finn). Expect compression whenever Wizards/EU allocation normalises or when many flippers discover the same table row. **Conclusion: real, quantified, but capacity-limited to low single-digit boxes per month per seller before Finn liquidity thins — it's a margin business, not a volume business.**
